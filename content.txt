period: 2025
images: canoe-cart-1.jpg, canoe-cart-2.jpg
illustrative: yes
note: Paid client project through Design Launchers, LLC. Due to NDA restrictions, images and proprietary design details have been omitted.
overview: A sheet metal canoe transport cart designed to carry a loaded kayak or canoe from vehicle to water, supporting up to 160 lbs across two large pneumatic drive wheels and two smaller stabilizing wheels. The hull rests on a rail cradle system secured with straps. The project was more technically constrained than the product category suggests; the entire structure is sheet metal, meaning every structural and geometric decision had to work within the limits of what can be bent, cut, and riveted from flat stock. End-user self-assembly was a hard requirement, which ruled out welding as a joining method and made rivet joint design and part alignment critical.
section: Bend radius and wall thickness management
- Balancing sheet thickness against required bend radii was the primary structural constraint throughout; thicker stock for load capacity, tighter radii for geometry, and the two work against each other
- Every bend feature was evaluated against minimum bend radius limits for the selected gauge to prevent cracking at the outer fiber
section: Corner and intersection workarounds
- Sheet metal corners at intersecting bend lines create material interference that cannot be resolved by bending alone
- Addressed through relief cuts and slit-notched corners that relieve the material before bending
- These features had to be sized correctly: too small and the bend still tears; too large and the corner loses structural integrity
- The workarounds were designed into the flat pattern from the start, not added as fixes after the geometry was set
section: Riveted construction for customer assembly
- First iteration used welding; redesigned to riveted construction to eliminate the need for specialized tools during customer assembly
- Rivet joint placement was driven by load path; joints at high-stress locations were sized and spaced for shear capacity, not just assembly convenience
- Part count minimized through geometry mirroring: two parts serve double duty on opposite sides of the assembly, reducing unique component count and simplifying the assembly sequence
section: Rail cradle system
- Designed the hull support rail geometry to accommodate canoe and kayak hull profiles without requiring hull-specific customization
- Strap attachment points integrated directly into the rail bends; no separate hardware required
outcome: Currently in manufacturing. Designed for self-assembly by the end user with no specialized tools. Full sheet metal flat pattern and production drawing package complete through Design Launchers, LLC.
