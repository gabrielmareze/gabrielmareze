note: Paid client project through Design Launchers, LLC. Due to NDA restrictions, images and proprietary design details have been omitted.
overview: A fleet of autonomous warehouse robots built for a client of Design Launchers. I worked on the physical design of the robot alongside the client’s engineering team, covering the chassis, the structure around the wheels, and the manufacturing drawings.
section: * Problem
- The robot rolled excessively in driving tests, and the drive wheels splayed outward under load, which caused positional drift.
section: * What I Did
- Traced the roll to an unbalanced 4-cell battery layout and undersized casters, and confirmed it with hand calculations and mass/CG simulations. Redesigned 15 components to fix it.
- Moved to a symmetric 2-2 battery split, which balanced the robot without adding structure or growing the footprint.
- Designed constraint brackets with auxiliary casters to take load off the drive wheels and stop the splay.
- Routed a C-section extrusion through the chassis to stiffen the wheel supports in a dense layout.
- Redlined 28 manufacturing prints and caught tolerance errors and a material mismatch before release.
outcome: 20% more payload than competing platforms at under $1,000 per unit. The client placed a follow-on order for 100 units.
skills: Root-cause analysis, Stability and center of gravity, GD&T and tolerance stacks, Design to cost, Manufacturing drawings
section: Takeaways
- Find the root cause before changing the design.
- A tight package decides where the structure can go.
- Check tolerance stacks early, before drawings are released.
